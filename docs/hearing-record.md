# What people said about the Homer City campus, against the scorecard

The scorecard ([SCORECARD.md](SCORECARD.md)) lists what a data center should be judged on. This
checks it against what people actually raised in the public record of the Homer City campus,
the same way pass 1 checked its site screen against sites developers really chose. Compiled
2026-11-16. Private individuals are counted by role only; officials by office.

## What it says

- **The records are of the gas plant, not the data centers.** The full public records found are
  DEP's hearings and comment responses on the gas plant and its pipeline. The county's two
  Planning Commission meetings on AWS's plan (2026-09-09 and 2026-10-14) have no public minutes,
  and DEP hasn't posted a transcript of its 2026-10-14 hearing on the creek discharge. Data
  centers came up in those records anyway.
- **What the scorecard covers, people do raise.** In the two full hearing transcripts (40
  speakers):
  - permanent jobs: 9, on both sides
  - water: 7
  - night sound: 6
  - power bills: 4
  - local taxes: 4
  - coal ash and land: 1

  Six of nine written comments on the Bruner Field grading pad (one of AWS's six sites) asked
  for testing of disturbed coal ash.
- **What people raise most is not on the scorecard:**
  - **Process and transparency:** 20 of 40 speakers, and 492 of 573 air-permit commenters on a
    rushed review.
  - **Stream ecology:** 20 of 40, plus every reported speaker at the October discharge hearing.
    They ask about degrees at the outfall and trout, not about reusing heat.
  - **The gas plant's air and health:** 15 of 40.
  - **Monitoring and enforcement:** 14 of 40.
  - **Who benefits:** 10 of 40.
  - **Private wells:** 7 of 40.
  - **Abandonment and cleanup liability:** 6 of 40.
  - **Local hiring and construction jobs:** 4 of 40, the supporters' main argument.
  - **Traffic and construction disruption** (Homer City's Main Street).
- **On the scorecard, raised by no one:** housing, hospital access, reuse of waste heat, and the
  data centers' own backup generators. Diesel came up only for the gas plant's 10 generators.
- **How the campus would look: not raised in any record found.** Lighting appears only in the
  county commissioners' own list of ordinance topics. That is an absence in incomplete records,
  not proof.
- **Low-frequency sound:** one speaker raised sound people can't hear, which dB(A) misses
  ([pass 2d](pass2/wall.md): walls do least there).
- **Most commenters were against:**
  - Hearing speakers: 3 for, 4 mixed or conditional, 33 against.
  - Air-permit commenters (DEP's count): 23 of 573 in support (4%).
  - Written comments on the county's ordinance amendment: 30 of 248 in support (12%). Of the
    248, 22% came from the three municipalities nearest the site; half came from Indiana borough
    and White Township.

  These counts are of who chose to comment, not of what residents think. Supporters spoke mostly
  at county meetings, whose minutes record names and topics only.

## What the scorecard should add (candidates, for Jon to decide)

1. **Disclosure and monitoring.** Publish the sound, water and heat numbers, with independent
   monitoring and alerts. No design number answers process complaints; disclosure is the part a
   design can control.
2. **The power that feeds the campus**: emissions of the gas plant per MWh delivered to the data
   centers. Residents tie the two together, and it is their main objection.
3. **Heat to the creek in degrees**: the temperature at the outfall and its effect on trout, not
   only the share of heat reused.
4. **Construction**: local hiring, traffic and disruption.
5. **Financial assurance**: who pays to clean up if the campus closes.
6. **Low-frequency sound** (dB(C) or octave bands) alongside dB(A).

## Sources

Full records:
- **The gas plant's air permit:** PA DEP's transcript of the hearing (2025-09-17, 13 speakers)
  and its comment-response document (573 commenters).
- **The gas pipeline's stormwater permit:** DEP's transcript (2026-05-12, 27 speakers) and its
  comment-response document (41 commenters).
- **Other DEP permits:** written comments on Bruner Field (permit E3206225-003, 9 submissions);
  comment responses on the power block's wetlands (14 commenters) and the Title V renewal (17).
- **The county:** the Indiana County commissioners' minutes, January-October 2026.

All the DEP records are on DEP's
[Homer City Generation Redevelopment page](https://www.pa.gov/agencies/dep/dep-regions/northwest-regional-office/homer-city-generation-redevelopment).
News (selected by reporters, so biased) from TribLive, WDAD, WJAC, WCCS, WESA, the
Tribune-Democrat and the Indiana Gazette. The comment-by-comment table is not published here:
some of its rows come from paywalled news still to be verified.

## What we don't know

- **The data-center venues:** the 2026-10-14 discharge hearing transcript, DEP's response
  document for that permit, the Planning Commission minutes and video, the 248 ordinance
  comments, and comments on AWS's plan through the county's online form. Any of these may change
  the picture. Each can be requested from DEP's Northwest Regional Office or the county planning
  office, or by a Right-to-Know request.
- **The next public meeting:** the Planning Commission meets on the AWS plan on 2026-11-18 at
  5 pm.
- **Local polls:** none found. Statewide polls aren't local opinion.
- **Conflicting reports:** sources disagree on the receiving stream (Blacklick Creek, Two Lick
  Creek, or their confluence) and the number of buildings (36 or 39). Resolve them before citing
  the permit.
